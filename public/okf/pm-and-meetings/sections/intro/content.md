**Software engineering** is a team sport. As projects scale, they naturally run into coordination challenges: dependencies arise, requirements change, and engineers get blocked. Without a structured way to coordinate, teams build the wrong things or waste effort.

However, the traditional solution — adding more project managers, scheduling more meetings, and demanding constant status updates — often backfires. This is known as **process overhead**.

Every meeting breaks an engineer's **maker time**. It takes significant cognitive effort to load complex codebases into mental memory; a single 30-minute meeting in the middle of the afternoon can destroy an entire half-day of focus. Moreover, **Parkinson's Law** states that work expands to fill the time allotted. If you schedule a 1-hour status meeting, it will take 1 hour, even if the actual status could be shared in 2 minutes of text.

To build software effectively, teams need **Lean Project Management** and **Async-First Meetings**. We must minimize meeting duration, limit attendance to essential members, protect focus blocks, and transition status updates to asynchronous channels.
